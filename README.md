# ShadowMap

**See your digital footprint. Take control of your privacy.**

ShadowMap is an interactive educational cybersecurity project that demonstrates how information shared across different online platforms can connect to reveal aspects of a person's digital footprint.

Through a fictional profile and an interactive relationship graph, users can explore how publicly shared information may create privacy risks and learn practical ways to protect their digital presence.

## 🎯 Project Mission

To help students and young people understand digital footprints, recognize potential privacy risks, and make more informed decisions about their online presence.

## ✨ Features

- **Interactive Digital Footprint Graph:** Explore connections between a fictional identity and different information categories.
- **Fictional Profile Builder:** Experiment with a fictional name, location, and interests.
- **Visual Relationship Mapping:** See how different categories of online information connect.
- **Privacy Risk Insights:** Understand how individual pieces of information may reveal additional context when combined.
- **Interactive Category Controls:** Add and remove information categories and explore their relationships.
- **Educational Explanations:** Learn about potential privacy risks and practical protection strategies.
- **Responsive Interface:** Use the platform on desktop and mobile devices.

## 🔐 Privacy and Ethical Principles

ShadowMap is designed as an educational demonstration.

- Uses fictional profile information by default.
- Does not scrape social media platforms.
- Does not collect real users' personal information through a backend.
- Does not track users or access private accounts.
- Demonstrates possible privacy risks rather than claiming to identify actual personal data.
- Encourages responsible and informed online behavior.

## ⚠️ Important Disclaimer

ShadowMap is an educational privacy-awareness tool, not a real-world surveillance or digital-footprint investigation platform.

Its visualizations are illustrative examples. They do not verify whether information is publicly available, identify actual individuals, or assess the security of real accounts.
